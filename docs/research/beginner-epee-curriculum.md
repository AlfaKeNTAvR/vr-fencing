# Beginner épée curriculum: WPI Fencing Club and a standard progression

Research for Linear PER-17. Researched 2026-10-18.

## Answer

- **WPI specifics are thin and old.** The club's only posted curriculum is a 7-week "Novice Training" list on fencing.wpi.edu, dated September 1997, plus a second-term list dated 2001. Both are **foil**, not épée: WPI starts novices on foil and moves them to épée or sabre after novice training. Nothing newer (practice plans, drill sheets) is public. The myWPI posts and news articles only cover logistics. ([beginners](https://fencing.wpi.edu/beginners.html), [beginners2](https://fencing.wpi.edu/beginners2.html), [WPI news 2019](https://www.wpi.edu/news/wpi-s-got-what-fencing-club))
- **WPI novice order (foil):** on guard, advance/retreat, lunge and step-lunge, grip, reprise, cross-step back, balestra, positions 6 and 4, engagement, parry 4, finger point control, disengage, parry 6, ripostes from 4 and 6, low-line attack, parry 8 and riposte, rules, judging, supervised bouting. Term 2 adds direct/indirect/compound attacks, the 1-2 and high-low, successive and circular parries, and beat.
- **A standard beginner épée progression** (Northwest Fencing Center's 2025 Beginner Épée Guide, the most complete public épée syllabus found) covers almost the same blocks in nearly the same order, plus épée-specific items: distance with whole-body target (hand, forearm, foot first), opposition, counter-attacks to hand/arm, remise/reprise, and parry 7.
- No public official USA Fencing or British Fencing beginner épée syllabus was found. British Fencing's Level 1 (ITCF) course lists outcomes, not a skill list.
- **For the VR app:** point control, extension, target accuracy (hand, forearm, body), parries 4/6/7/8 and riposte, disengage, opposition, and counter-attacks to the arm all suit one tracked controller. Footwork (advance, retreat, lunge, distance) only works in VR as head-translation estimates, so treat it as "rough feedback", not form coaching.

## Ordered skill list

Order merges the WPI novice list (foil) with the NWFC Beginner Épée Guide. "VR solo" column:
- **Good**: the skill is mostly hand/blade (controller pose and timing) and can be scored well.
- **Partial**: needs legs or body; only estimable from headset height/translation plus controller.
- **No**: needs a partner, a referee or real equipment, or is knowledge only.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Safety, salute, equipment | Salute before/after each bout (point up, guard to chin, point down); tip down when not fencing. | Partial (salute is a controller gesture; safety is knowledge) | NWFC p.2-3 |
| 2 | Grip and weapon handling | French grip: thumb and index pinch near the guard and steer the point, other fingers aid. Pommel along the forearm. | Partial (controller is not a French grip; can only coach wrist alignment) | NWFC p.3, WPI #5 |
| 3 | En garde (on guard) | Base stance for balance and mobility. | Partial (head height and weapon-hand position checkable, feet not) | NWFC p.4, WPI #2 |
| 4 | Advance / retreat | Front foot leads going forward, back foot leads going back. | Partial (head translation along the piste axis; foot order not visible) | NWFC p.4, WPI #3 |
| 5 | Extension (arm first) | Straighten the weapon arm, point at target, before the feet move. | Good (controller reach and timing relative to head motion) | NWFC p.4 (lunge "starts with the front arm extending") |
| 6 | Lunge and recovery | Arm extends, front foot kicks forward, back leg pushes; recover by bending back knee and pushing off front leg. | Partial (head drop and forward jump plus arm-first timing; leg shape unseen) | NWFC p.4, WPI #4 |

| 10 | Target areas (épée: whole body) | Hand and front foot are near targets; elbow and thigh mid; body furthest, needs commitment. | 12 | Engagement (covered / engaged) | Blade contact and closing your line against the opponent's blade. | Partial (needs an opponent blade; floating blade in later milestone) | WPI #10 |
| 13 | Point control with fingers | Small precise tip movements from thumb and index. | Good (tip accuracy on small targets; controller is held differently from a real grip) | WPI #12, NWFC p.3 |
| 14 | Straight (direct) attack | Simple attack, one tip motion, arm extended, moving forward, starts and ends in the same line. | Good | NWFC p.7 |
| 15 | Disengage (indirect attack) | Tip changes line with finger work: under the opponent's blade in high lines, over in low lines. | Good once a virtual opponent blade exists | NWFC p.7, WPI #13 |
| 16 | Attack with opposition | Hit while leaning on the opponent's blade to close the line, avoiding double touches. | Partial (needs blade-on-blade physics with no haptic resistance) | NWFC p.7 |
| 17 | Parry 4 then parry 6 (lateral) | Deflect with the strong part of your blade on their weak, tip staying near target. | Good (floating opponent blade) | NWFC p.8, WPI #11, #14 |
| 18 | Parry-riposte | Hit back right after the parry, with or without opposition. | Good | NWFC p.8, WPI #15 |
| 19 | Parry 8 (and 7) and riposte; low-line attack | Semi-circular parries to low lines; attacks to low targets (thigh, knee). | Good | NWFC p.6, p.8, WPI #16-17 |
| 20 | Defence by distance | Retreat so the attack falls short, then attack immediately. | Partial (head retreat measurable) | NWFC p.8 |
| 21 | Remise / reprise | Renewed hit straight after a failed one: remise in the same line, reprise in a new line. Always follow a body attack with one. | Good (blade); reprise with footwork Partial | NWFC p.8 |
| 22 | Counter-attack to hand/arm | Keep your arm above theirs, aim at hand or elbow, move in and out of distance and be ready to retreat. | Good (épée-specific; core solo drill against an extending virtual arm) | NWFC p.8 |
| 23 | Compound attacks: feint-deceive, one-two, doublé | Feint draws a parry, then disengage(s) around it: one-two against two lateral parries, doublé against circular. | Good with a reactive virtual blade (later milestone) | NWFC p.7, WPI term 2 |
| 24 | Circular and successive parries | Circular: start and end in the same position. Successive: chain of parries. | Good | NWFC p.8, WPI term 2 |
| 25 | Beat | Sharp tap on the opponent's blade to open a line or prepare an attack. | Partial (no haptic contact) | WPI term 2, NWFC p.5 (thigh hit "set-up with a feint or a beat") |
| 26 | Rules, strip, commands, penalties, judging | Off-end touch, side-out, commands, yellow/red cards; double touches in épée (both within 40 ms). | No (knowledge; could be a quiz) | NWFC p.5, p.9, WPI #18-19 |
| 27 | Supervised free fencing (bouting) | Real bouts. | No | WPI #20 |

### Notes for drill design

- WPI teaches foil first, so its order puts parries 4/6 before point-to-hand work. The NWFC épée guide puts distance and whole-body targets (hand, forearm) early, which matches épée having no right of way: whoever hits first scores ([fencer.com parries handout](https://fencer.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/Parries.pdf)). For an épée app, target drills to hand/forearm/body (items 5, 10, 13, 14) are the natural first block, and they need only the controller.
- Parries (17-19, 24) and blade actions (15, 16, 23, 25) need the floating opponent blade milestone.
- Leg-dependent skills (4, 6-9, 20) can get rough feedback from headset motion (forward translation, head drop on lunge, return on recovery), not form correction.

## Gaps

- No current (post-2001) WPI curriculum, practice plan or drill sheet is public. Instagram and the Facebook group were not readable without login. The coach (Dave Brown, dcb@wpi.edu) or club officers are the only route to the current plan.
- The WPI site lists general learning links (e.g. fencing.net drills), but not as part of novice training.
- No official USA Fencing beginner épée syllabus is public. British Fencing's ITCF (Level 1) course page lists coaching outcomes only.
